Remember: A reference point is a place or object used to describe an object's position or motion.

1. Distance vs. Displacement

Both describe movement, but they answer different questions.

Distance

  • The total length of the path travelled.
  • A scalar quantity: it has magnitude only.
  • Always zero or positive.

Example: You walk 3 km to a store and 3 km back home.

Distance = 3 km + 3 km = 6 km

Displacement

  • The straight-line change from starting point to ending point.
  • A vector: it has magnitude and direction.
  • Can be zero if you return to where you started.

Example: If you finish at the store, 3 km east of home, your displacement is 3 km east.

Displacement = 3 km east

Key idea: If you walk 3 km east and then 3 km west back to your starting point, your distance is 6 km but your displacement is 0 km.

Guided example

A cyclist travels 5 km north, then 2 km south. Find the total distance and displacement.

  • Distance = 5 + 2 = 7 km
  • Displacement = 5 km north − 2 km south = 3 km north
Try it: Ana walks 4 m east, then 4 m west. What are her distance and displacement?

2. Speed vs. Velocity

Speed tells how fast. Velocity tells how fast and in what direction.

Speed

  • How fast an object moves.
  • Scalar quantity; no direction.
  • Common units: m/s and km/h.
Speed = Distance ÷ Time

Example: A learner runs 100 m in 20 s.

Speed = 100 m ÷ 20 s = 5 m/s

Velocity

  • Speed in a stated direction.
  • Vector quantity; magnitude and direction.
  • Direction may be north, south, east, west, etc.
Velocity = Displacement ÷ Time

Example: A car's displacement is 60 km east in 2 h.

Velocity = 60 km east ÷ 2 h = 30 km/h east

Compare: “A bus moves at 40 km/h” describes speed. “A bus moves at 40 km/h south” describes velocity.

Worked problem

A student walks 150 m east in 50 seconds.

  1. Average speed = total distance ÷ time = 150 ÷ 50 = 3 m/s.
  2. Average velocity = displacement ÷ time = 150 m east ÷ 50 s = 3 m/s east.
Quick check: Which statement describes velocity?

3. Graphing Motion

A distance-time graph shows how the distance of an object changes as time passes.

How to read the graph

  • Time is usually on the horizontal (x) axis.
  • Distance is usually on the vertical (y) axis.
  • A straight sloping line shows constant speed.
  • A steeper line means faster speed.
  • A horizontal line means the object is stationary.
  • A curved line can show changing speed.

Example: uniform motion

A toy car travels 20 m every 4 seconds.

The straight line has a constant slope: 5 m/s.

Explore the graph

Move the slider to change the car's speed. Observe how the line changes.

4 m/s

A 4 m/s car travels 40 m in 10 seconds.

Uniform motion

Equal distances are travelled in equal time intervals. A distance-time graph is a straight sloping line.

Stationary object

The distance does not change as time passes. A distance-time graph is a horizontal line.

Interpret: A distance-time graph is a straight line from 0 m at 0 s to 60 m at 10 s. What is the speed?

4. Identifying and Controlling Variables

A fair investigation changes one factor, measures the result, and keeps other important factors the same.

Independent variable

The factor the investigator deliberately changes. It is the possible cause.

Question: What do I change?

Dependent variable

The factor that is measured or observed. It is the result.

Question: What do I measure?

Controlled variables

The factors kept the same so the test is fair.

Question: What must stay the same?

Why control variables?

They help make results fair, accurate, and reliable so we can tell what caused the change.

Investigation example: Does ramp height affect a toy car's speed?

Type of variableIn this investigation
IndependentHeight of the ramp
DependentSpeed of the toy car (measured over a set distance)
ControlledSame toy car, same ramp surface, same starting point, same distance, same release method
Fair-test tip: Change only the ramp height. If you also change the car or ramp surface, you will not know which change affected the speed.

  1. What total distance did Ben travel?
